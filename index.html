About

Yoshihara Group

I am an Assistant Professor of Physics at the University of Hawaii at Mānoa. I work on collider experiments (SuperKEKB/Belle II) to search for physics beyond the Standard Model. Previously, I was a member of the ATLAS experiment at CERN.

My group also develops detector and readout systems, including low-latency AI/ML on hardware.

    Seminar details (text version of the flyer)

    ICS 690 Seminar, Information & Computer Sciences
    Friday, September 4, 2026, 12:30–1:30 PM, AgSci 220

    Speaker: Keisuke Yoshihara, Assistant Professor, Physics & Astronomy, University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa. Ph.D., University of Tokyo. Currently working on SuperKEKB/Belle II; previously worked on the LHC-ATLAS experiment at CERN.

    Abstract: Modern particle physics experiments are among the world’s most demanding computing systems, processing enormous data streams in real time. Experiments such as Belle II require detector information to be analyzed within only a few microseconds for event triggering and data acquisition. This seminar introduces the computing challenges behind modern collider experiments and presents recent developments in FPGA-based real-time computing, AI acceleration on hardware, and AI-agent systems for chip design and scientific data analysis. The talk will also highlight opportunities for interdisciplinary collaboration between computer science and experimental physics through research and education at UH.

    Research themes: real-time systems, FPGA acceleration, AI agents, scientific data.
